Project Overview

The Girls in Science (GiS) project was a groundbreaking STEM education initiative that established science clubs in 13 government schools in Baghlan Province, Afghanistan. Targeting Grade 9–12 girls, the project aimed to increase access to quality science education, build STEM skills, and influence Ministry of Education policy on girls' science education nationally.

International Achievement — Bronze Medals at Qatar Science Olympiad

Four GiS girls were selected to represent Afghanistan at the International Junior Science Olympiad (IJSO) in Qatar, competing with participants from 75 countries. They were awarded bronze medals — a remarkable achievement demonstrating the program's impact on girls' STEM capacity in conflict-affected Afghanistan.

Evaluation Findings by Theme
Interest & Motivation
99.7% found science club activities fun and useful for understanding science. Interest was so high that club membership grew beyond planned capacity from 25 to 30 students per school.
Quality Preparation
Girls acquired presentation skills and presented to communities and at international competitions. Volunteer teachers received targeted quality preparation support with measurable results.
Community Involvement
Parents initially reluctant shifted to strong supporters after seeing daughters' achievements. Community attitudes toward girls' science education changed significantly during project lifecycle.
Policy Influence
All 6 MoE officials interviewed had attended GiS activities. Project modelled on School Student Associations (SSA) structure to ensure MoE alignment and potential national scale-up.
Career Aspirations
95.3% of GiS participants expressed interest in pursuing STEM careers — a transformational shift in a context where girls' secondary education was being increasingly restricted.
Sustainability
GiS modelled on existing SSA structures to promote institutionalization. Ongoing DED and PED involvement provided pathway to sustained government ownership beyond project cycle.
