Job Intelligence Portal: how I automated my own job search

I designed and built a system that tracks 638 organizations, mostly in the DC, Maryland and Virginia area, checks their career pages automatically, screens every new job for fit and eligibility, emails me a summary each evening at 7 p.m., and tracks every application in one private web portal.

Why I built it

When I started my job search in the DC area, I had no one to guide me. I applied through Indeed, Glassdoor and LinkedIn, mostly Indeed and Glassdoor. By the time I saw a job, it had often been posted 15, 20 or 25 days earlier, and many organizations were already close to hiring. Most of my applications got no reply at all, not even a receipt, and some got a rejection email weeks later.

So I started applying directly on employers' own websites, and it worked. I was shortlisted and invited to seven interviews. Three of them ended because the organization cancelled or paused the position. But checking dozens of career pages by hand every day took hours.

I treated it like any other data problem. If the right jobs appear first on employers' own websites, the answer is a system that checks those websites for me every day and tells me only about the jobs that fit.

What it does

  1. 1Keeps a watch list. A Google Sheet lists 638 organizations I want to work for: Maryland, county and DC government, nonprofits, international development organizations, research institutes, universities and consulting firms.
  2. 2Finds each job feed. The script detects the hiring system behind each career page (Workday, Oracle, ADP, iCIMS, Greenhouse, Lever, GovernmentJobs, Maryland JobAps, USAJOBS, ReliefWeb and more) and tests it. Plain career pages are watched for changes. 172 career sites are connected so far, and more are added as their feeds are found. Some sites block automated checks, and the sheet notes which ones.
  3. 3Checks on a schedule. High priority employers are checked every run and others less often, so it stays within Google's free limits.
  4. 4Scores each title. Weighted patterns score titles such as data analyst, MEL, evaluation, research and assessment, and drop internships and unrelated roles.
  5. 5Screens eligibility. It reads the job description and blocks jobs that require a security clearance or US citizenship, while keeping Public Trust positions. When the wording is unclear, it marks the job "Needs review" and shows the exact sentence instead of guessing.
  6. 6Removes duplicates when the same job appears through two sources.
  7. 7Emails me at 7 p.m. with the day's new matches, each linked to its page in the portal.
  8. 8Tracks my applications. In the portal I save, skip or mark jobs as applied, set follow-up dates and keep notes. Every change is logged.

How I designed it

I designed it the way I design monitoring systems for programs: one trusted source of data, clear rules, an audit trail and a person making the final call.

One source of truth

The Google Sheet is the only database. The email, the portal and the scheduled checks all read and write the same tables.

Safe updates

Every job has a stable ID. Updates use that ID and a lock, so sorting the sheet or editing in two places never changes the wrong row. Outdated edits are refused.

Human in the loop

When I skip a job with a clear reason, the system proposes a filtering rule. Nothing is filtered until I approve it, and I can preview what a rule would hide.

Transparent screening

Each eligibility decision shows the sentence it is based on. Unclear cases go to a "Needs review" list instead of being hidden or sent as eligible.

Built to run for free

Priority tiers, batching and a runtime limit keep it inside free Gmail and Apps Script quotas. A health panel shows which sites failed and why.

Tested

About 30 automated tests use fake job sites and a fake mailer to check matching, eligibility, duplicates, alerts and the portal, without touching real data.

What changed for me

  • In its first day, it read about 11,000 job postings and saved about 550 that fit my profile for review.
  • I now see relevant jobs on the day they are posted, instead of two or three weeks later on job boards.
  • One email at 7 p.m. replaces hours of checking career pages by hand.
  • Jobs I cannot apply for, such as those that require a security clearance, are filtered out before I spend time on them.
  • Every application, follow-up date and note is in one place, so nothing slips.

What it shows about my work

The same skills apply to any organization's data work: mapping messy sources, writing clear rules, automating routine checks, keeping an audit trail and building a simple tool that people actually use. I can build this kind of system for program monitoring, grant tracking or reporting pipelines.
