Program Overview

UNESCO launched a radio-based learning program to address the urgent educational needs in Afghanistan — one of the most educationally deprived contexts in the world. The program provided Basic General Literacy (BGL) to 4,600 adults with limited literacy skills, and alternative secondary education (Grade 10–12) to approximately 40,000 out-of-school adolescent girls and boys who could not access formal schooling due to Taliban restrictions or geographic barriers.

Radio-based delivery was chosen as the primary modality because it could reach learners in remote communities, was culturally acceptable, and could provide access to girls and women who faced Taliban-imposed restrictions on movement and formal education.

Evaluation Methodology

A rigorous mixed-methods approach was applied, combining structured quantitative surveys with qualitative KIIs, IDIs, FGDs, and direct radio station observations.

Quantitative Methods
  • Structured on-site surveys with literacy learners
  • Remote surveys with Grade 10–12 learners
  • In-person radio station infrastructure observations
  • Learner quiz performance analysis (Math, Language, Quran, Sciences)
  • Gender and province-disaggregated analysis
Qualitative Methods
  • Remote Key Informant Interviews (KIIs)
  • In-Depth Interviews (IDIs)
  • In-person Focus Group Discussions (FGDs)
  • Human-interest / change stories
  • Desk review of program documentation

OECD/DAC Criteria Findings
Relevance ✓ Strong
Reached the most vulnerable groups. Radio-based approach overcame cultural and geographic barriers. All learners confirmed lessons covered important subjects.
Coherence ✓ Strong
Complemented formal schooling without creating overlap. Flexible model accommodated women with household responsibilities. Filled critical gap where formal schooling was unavailable.
Effectiveness ✓ Moderate-Strong
1/3 of learners reported significant literacy improvement; 2/3 reported some improvement. Radio lessons proved effective even without in-person trainer support.
Efficiency ✓ Good
Successfully delivered educational content at scale. Majority found broadcast timings suitable. Most radio stations had good infrastructure and signal coverage.
Impact ✓ Positive
78% reported some life skills improvement; 22% significant improvement. Improved literacy helped men engage in business. More positive community attitude toward female education reported.
Sustainability ⚠ At Risk
Uncertain without support structures (books, in-person visits). Radio stations need financial support. Short project duration limited sustainable gains.

"Radio-based delivery of education has proved to be an effective mechanism for meeting the educational needs of marginalized communities in Afghan provinces with restricted access to education, particularly for women and girls." — UNESCO Final Evaluation Report, April 2025
